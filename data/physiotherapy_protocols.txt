PHYSIOTHERAPY REHABILITATION PROTOCOLS
=======================================
Sports Medicine Rehabilitation Reference
Source: Evidence-Based Physiotherapy Practice Guidelines


SECTION 1: PRINCIPLES OF SPORTS REHABILITATION
================================================

The Rehabilitation Continuum
Sports rehabilitation follows a continuum from acute injury management through
to full return to sport. The process can be divided into four main phases,
each with specific goals, exercises, and criteria that must be met before
progression to the next phase.

Criteria-Based vs Time-Based Progression
Modern sports rehabilitation uses criteria-based progression rather than
rigid time frames. An athlete progresses when they meet specific functional
milestones, not simply because a set number of weeks has passed. This
reduces the risk of premature return and re-injury.

Pain as a Guide
Pain should be used as a guide during rehabilitation:
0–3 / 10: Acceptable during exercise, usually indicates healthy loading
4–6 / 10: Caution — reduce load, monitor next-day response
7+ / 10: Too much — stop the activity, rest, and reassess

Post-exercise soreness that resolves within 24 hours is generally acceptable.
Increased baseline pain or night pain requires physiotherapy review.


SECTION 2: ANKLE SPRAIN REHABILITATION PROTOCOL
================================================

Phase 1 — Acute Phase (Days 1–5)
Goals: Reduce swelling and pain, protect the tissue, maintain fitness.

Exercises:
- Ankle pumps (slow dorsiflexion and plantarflexion, 20 reps, 4x/day)
- Gentle ankle circles within pain-free range (10 each direction, 3x/day)
- Isometric exercises with a resistance band (4 directions, 10s holds x 10)
- Non-weight-bearing upper body and contralateral limb conditioning

Criteria to progress: Swelling reducing, able to partially bear weight
without significant pain.

Phase 2 — Sub-Acute Phase (Days 5–21)
Goals: Restore full range of motion, normalise gait, begin weight bearing.

Exercises:
- Standing calf raises (double leg, progress to single leg): 3 x 15–20 reps
- Balance board or wobble board standing (2 x 60 seconds)
- Theraband resistance exercises in all planes: 3 x 15 reps
- Pool walking or cycling if running is not yet possible
- Gentle hamstring, calf, and quadriceps stretching
- Progress to normal walking on flat surfaces

Criteria to progress: Full range of motion, normal gait pattern, able to
single-leg stand for 30 seconds with minimal wobble, minimal swelling.

Phase 3 — Functional Phase (Weeks 3–8)
Goals: Restore strength, proprioception, and sport-specific movement.

Exercises:
- Single-leg calf raises: 3 x 15–20 reps
- Single-leg squat progressions: 3 x 10–15 reps
- Lateral shuffle drills, figure-of-eight walking
- Agility ladder drills (flat, progressing to cutting movements)
- Hop and land progressions: double leg → single leg
- Proprioception challenges: Eyes-open → eyes-closed standing

Criteria to progress: Single-leg hop distance ≥90% of uninjured side,
able to jog without pain, full strength compared to uninjured side.

Phase 4 — Return to Sport (Weeks 6–12)
Goals: Restore sport-specific fitness and confidence.

Exercises:
- Straight-line jogging → increasing speeds → sprinting
- Cutting, side-stepping, change of direction drills
- Sport-specific skill work
- Full training drills with ankle brace for first 4–6 weeks back

Return to full competition criteria:
- No pain with sport-specific activities
- Full strength and range of motion
- Passes functional hop tests (≥90% symmetry)
- Confident in the joint — no fear of re-injury


SECTION 3: HAMSTRING STRAIN REHABILITATION PROTOCOL
====================================================

Phase 1 — Acute Phase (Days 1–7)
Goals: Reduce pain and bleeding, protect the muscle, maintain fitness.

- PRICE protocol
- Isometric hamstring contractions at a comfortable length (5s holds, 10 reps)
- Stationary cycling (no resistance) if comfortable
- Upper body strength work and contralateral leg training

Do NOT stretch the hamstring in this phase.

Phase 2 — Early Rehabilitation (Week 1–3)
Goals: Restore range of motion, begin progressive loading.

- Prone hip extension with knee bent (gentle): 3 x 15 reps
- Bridging progressions: double → single-leg: 3 x 10 reps
- Leg curl on machine (low load): 3 x 15 reps
- Gentle stationary cycling with progressive resistance
- Begin walking at comfortable pace

Phase 3 — Mid-Rehabilitation (Weeks 3–6)
Goals: Strengthen through full range, build running tolerance.

- Nordic hamstring curls (eccentric focus): 2 x 8–12 reps
- Romanian deadlifts with controlled load: 3 x 10–12 reps
- Single-leg deadlifts: 3 x 10 each side
- Progressive running programme: walk → jog → run (no sprinting yet)
  Follow a structured running volume plan, increasing 10–15% per week

Phase 4 — Return to Sport (Weeks 6–12)
Goals: Full speed running, sport-specific movement, return to competition.

- Sprint drills at progressively increasing intensity
- Cutting and change of direction at speed
- Sport-specific training sessions
- Maintain Nordic hamstring exercise as prevention 2x/week

Return to competition criteria:
- No pain with maximal speed running
- Strength ≥90% of uninjured side on isokinetic testing or single-leg tests
- Full confidence in the muscle
- Passed high-speed running tolerance test

Prevention after hamstring strain:
The Nordic hamstring curl (eccentric) programme significantly reduces the risk
of hamstring re-injury and should be continued as part of regular training.


SECTION 4: ACL REHABILITATION PROTOCOL (POST-SURGERY)
=======================================================

Overview
ACL reconstruction rehabilitation is a long, demanding process typically
spanning 9–12 months. Premature return to sport is the primary risk factor
for graft failure and re-injury.

Month 1–2: Early Phase
Goals: Reduce swelling, restore range of motion, activate quadriceps.

- Ankle pumps, straight-leg raises, isometric quadriceps activation
- Stationary cycling (low resistance) — start Day 10 post-op if cleared
- Restore full knee extension: critical in this phase
- Swelling management: elevation, ice, compression
- No knee flexion beyond 90° for the first 4–6 weeks (surgeon-dependent)

Month 2–4: Strengthening Phase
Goals: Regain strength, walking without aids, begin cardiovascular training.

- Leg press (bilateral): start at 50% body weight, progress
- Mini-squats (0–45°): 3 x 15 reps
- Step-ups and step-downs: 3 x 12 reps
- Single-leg balance and proprioception
- Pool running or swimming for cardiovascular fitness
- Target: Full extension, flexion to 120° by week 12

Month 4–6: Neuromuscular Phase
Goals: Build strength to ≥70% symmetry, introduce running.

- Progressive squats and lunges
- Hip strengthening programme (hip abductors and external rotators)
- Running programme begins if quadriceps symmetry ≥60% on testing
  Walk: jog: run progression over 4 weeks
- Avoid jumping and pivoting until Month 6 clearance

Month 6–9: Functional Phase
Goals: Sport-specific movements, plyometrics, agility.

- Cutting and change of direction drills at progressive intensities
- Plyometric programme: box jumps, lateral hops, bounding
- Agility ladder, cone drills
- Return to non-contact sport-specific training

Month 9–12: Return to Sport Phase
Goals: Full return to competitive sport.

Criteria for return to competition (must meet ALL):
- Quadriceps strength ≥90% of uninjured side (isokinetic testing preferred)
- Hamstring:Quadriceps ratio within normal range
- Single-leg hop test battery ≥90% symmetry
- Y-balance test within normal limits
- Psychological readiness (ACL-RSI score ≥65%)
- Medical clearance from treating surgeon and physiotherapist
- No pain or swelling with high-intensity training

Important: Skipping phases or returning before meeting criteria significantly
increases the risk of ACL graft re-rupture and future knee problems.


SECTION 5: ROTATOR CUFF REHABILITATION PROTOCOL
================================================

Phase 1 — Acute Phase (Weeks 1–3)
Goals: Reduce pain and inflammation, maintain shoulder mobility.

- Pendulum exercises (Codman exercises): 3 x 10, 3x/day
- Passive and active-assisted range of motion exercises
- Isometric external and internal rotation: 10s holds x 10 reps
- Ice for 15 minutes after exercise
- Postural correction exercises (scapular retraction and depression)

Phase 2 — Strengthening Phase (Weeks 3–8)
Goals: Restore rotator cuff strength and scapular control.

- Theraband external rotation: 3 x 15 reps
- Theraband internal rotation: 3 x 15 reps
- Side-lying external rotation with light dumbbell: 3 x 12–15 reps
- Scapular stability exercises: serratus anterior activation, wall slides
- Avoid overhead pressing exercises in this phase

Phase 3 — Advanced Strengthening (Weeks 8–16)
Goals: Progressive overhead loading, sport-specific strength.

- Overhead pressing with progressive load
- Lat pull-down
- Cable external and internal rotation at varying heights
- Plyometric ball throws at low intensity
- Sport-specific throwing or serving drills at reduced intensity

Phase 4 — Return to Sport (Weeks 12–24)
Goals: Full return to sport-specific activity.

- Progressive throwing/serving programmes
- Field/court based sport-specific drills
- Full training intensity

Criteria: Full pain-free range of motion, strength ≥90% of uninjured side,
able to complete all sport-specific drills without pain.


SECTION 6: CONCUSSION RETURN TO SPORT PROTOCOL
===============================================

Key Principles
1. The athlete must be completely symptom-free AT REST before starting the
   return to sport (RTS) protocol.
2. Each step takes a minimum of 24 hours.
3. If symptoms return at any step, return to the previous step and wait a
   minimum of 24 hours before attempting again.
4. The entire protocol must be supervised by a healthcare professional.
5. Children and adolescents take longer to recover and should follow a
   modified protocol with their school/education also involved.

Step 1: Symptom-limited daily activity
Low-intensity cognitive and physical tasks that do not provoke symptoms.
Activities: Walking, light housework, reading for short periods.

Step 2: Light aerobic exercise
Goal: Increase heart rate without any symptom provocation.
Activities: Walking, swimming, stationary cycling. No resistance training.
No head-jolting movements.

Step 3: Sport-specific exercise
Goal: Add movement patterns relevant to the sport.
Activities: Running drills, skating drills (no contact or risk of head impact).

Step 4: Non-contact training drills
Goal: Exercise, co-ordination, and cognitive load.
Activities: More complex drills, passing drills, resistance training begins.
Medical clearance required before this step.

Step 5: Full-contact practice
Goal: Restore confidence and assess functional skills by coaching staff.
Medical clearance REQUIRED before this step.
Activities: Normal training activities with full contact permitted.

Step 6: Return to competition
Normal game play.

Medical Clearance Requirement
A qualified medical doctor (or sports medicine physician) must provide
written clearance before the athlete progresses to Steps 5 and 6.

Important Note on Second Impact Syndrome
Second Impact Syndrome (SIS) occurs when a second concussion is sustained
before the first has fully resolved. It is rare but can be catastrophic or
fatal due to rapid brain swelling. This is why the "if in doubt, sit them out"
rule is non-negotiable.
